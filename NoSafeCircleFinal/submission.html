No Safe Circle

A tiny wizard is lost in a dungeon. Something is down there with you, and five doors stand between you and the way out.

The Run

Doors open on a timer. Click one and your wizard walks over and works on it; it swings open, and once you step through it slams and locks behind you. There is no going back.

Enemies hunt you. Let one reach you and it hits you, repeatedly — your health is shown in the top-left. A fireball is cheaper than a fight, and you have no melee attack. A door between you and something chasing you is an escape, not just progress.

You escape by crossing the final door. You die when your health reaches zero. Either way, press R to start over.

How It Was Built

The game was produced through a multi-agent AI development pipeline rather than by hand. The pipeline treats the project as a validated task graph and routes work across providers:

  • Task graph as the source of truth. Ninety-three task contracts with parent and dependency edges, exclusive file-ownership claims, and resource groups, all checked by a validator that enforces an acyclic graph and single ownership per file.
  • Contract review across two providers. A generate–evaluate–refine cycle in which one model authors a contract revision, an independent model audits it, the author refines, and a fresh auditor re-checks before anything is committed.
  • Decomposition with an independent reviewer. Oversized tasks are split by one provider and reviewed by another; a proposal is review-only until a human approves the exact plan.
  • Execution and test gates. Implementation work runs in containerized provider sessions against Unity Edit Mode and Play Mode test suites, with a non-mutation rule: a passing suite that leaves the repository dirty is a failed run.
  • Human approval at the boundaries. Visual quality, design decisions, and anything that changes the graph remain human-gated.

Scope and Honesty

The submitted build is a vertical slice: five connected rooms, a door sequence with a real locking rule, an enemy that pursues, a cursor-aimed fireball, and explicit win and loss states. Character and enemy art in this build are placeholder silhouettes; generated pixel-art sets exist in the repository but are not wired into this build.
